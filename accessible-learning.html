LEARNING FOR EVERYONE

Accessible Learning

Learning becomes more manageable when information is clearly organized and presented in different ways. Small changes can help students understand and use educational materials more effectively.

What Is Accessible Learning?

Accessible learning means presenting educational information in ways that different students can use. It considers how people read, understand, interact with, and respond to learning materials.

Some students benefit from shorter explanations, while others may find diagrams, audio or practical examples helpful. Offering different formats can make learning more flexible.

The goal is not to make learning less meaningful. It is to reduce unnecessary barriers so students can focus on understanding the subject.

Make Written Information Clearer

Use Clear Headings

Divide information into sections with headings that explain what each part is about.

Keep Paragraphs Focused

Present one main idea at a time instead of placing too much information in one paragraph.

Choose Readable Formatting

Use readable text, enough spacing, and consistent formatting throughout the page.

Explain Unfamiliar Words

Introduce difficult terms in simple language and give examples when they are useful.

Learn in Different Ways

Written explanations are useful, but they are not the only way to present information. Depending on the topic and the student's needs, other formats can also support understanding.

Visual Materials

Pictures, diagrams, charts and mind maps can help explain connections between ideas.

Audio Materials

Recorded explanations and text-to-speech can provide another way to access written content.

Practical Examples

Demonstrations and examples can help connect abstract information with real situations.

Break Tasks into Smaller Steps

Long instructions can feel difficult to follow. Dividing a task into smaller steps helps show what needs to be done and in what order.

  1. Understand the task

    Read the instructions and identify the main goal.

  2. Prepare the information

    Gather the materials and key ideas you need.

  3. Work through each step

    Complete one part before moving on to the next.

  4. Review your work

    Check the result against the original instructions.

Tips for Students

Organize your notes

Use headings, lists and short summaries to make important information easier to find later.

Choose tools that help you

Try reading aloud, audio materials or visual notes and see which options work well for your tasks.

Ask for clarification

If an instruction is confusing, ask for an example or request that it be explained in smaller steps.

Tips for Teachers

Give Clear Instructions

Explain tasks in a logical order and highlight the most important requirements.

Offer Different Formats

Where possible, provide text alongside useful diagrams, audio or demonstrations.

Allow Questions

Give students opportunities to clarify instructions and check their understanding.

Explore Digital Support

Digital tools can provide additional ways to read, write and work with educational information. Explore some of the options available.
